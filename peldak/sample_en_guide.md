# Revision guide: peldak/sample_en.txt

The text has 23 flagged places in 107 words (215.0 per 1,000 words), under 15 kinds of task.

This guide gives instructions; it does not rewrite the text. Every flag is a question: if a phrase belongs to the genre or is deliberate, it can stay.

## 1. Tasks in order of priority

### 1. Rewrite or delete generic conclusions

3 places · fix · rule: `generic-conclusion`

**Why it is a problem:** Upbeat generalities would fit the end of any text, so they say nothing about this one.

**How to fix it:**

1. Delete the sentence and see whether anything is missing.
2. If you need a close, be specific: what follows, what remains open.

**Where:**

- line 5: 'Despite these challenges'
- line 5: 'continues to evolve'
- line 5: 'paving the way for'

### 2. Cut filler lead-ins

2 places · fix · rule: `filler`

**Why it is a problem:** The phrase announces that something matters instead of saying it.

**How to fix it:**

1. Delete the phrase up to and including 'that'.
2. Start the sentence with what followed it.
3. If the sentence now feels weak, strengthen the content, not the wrapper.

**Where:**

- line 1: 'It is important to note that'
- line 1: 'In today's fast-paced world'

### 3. Cut trailing '-ing' commentary

2 places · fix · rule: `ing-tail`

**Why it is a problem:** The tacked-on participle ('…, highlighting the importance of…') asserts significance without showing it.

**How to fix it:**

1. End the sentence before the comma.
2. If the consequence is real, give it its own sentence and say how you know.
3. If it is only commentary, delete it.

**Where:**

- line 3: ', highlighting the importance of rapid feedback'
- line 5: ', paving the way for new possibilities'

### 4. Replace promotional adjectives with facts

2 places · fix · rule: `promo`

**Why it is a problem:** Advertising language makes a neutral claim less credible.

**How to fix it:**

1. Say what makes the thing different: a number, an example, a comparison.
2. If you cannot, delete the adjective.

**Where:**

- line 1: 'innovative'
- line 1: 'seamless'

### 5. Shorten wordy connectives

2 places · fix · rule: `wordy`

**Why it is a problem:** Several words stand where one would do, and the sentence slows down.

**How to fix it:**

1. Swap: 'in order to' → 'to'; 'due to the fact that' → 'because'; 'in the event that' → 'if'; 'is able to' → 'can'; 'a number of' → 'several'; 'utilize' → 'use'.
2. Reread: the meaning must not change.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'are able to'
- line 3: 'in order to'

### 6. Delete leftover chatbot phrasing

1 places · fix · rule: `chatbot-residue`

**Why it is a problem:** These sentences frame a chat reply; in finished prose they show the text was not read through.

**How to fix it:**

1. Delete the opening and closing courtesy sentences.
2. Check the first and last lines of the text: they hide there.

**Where:**

- line 5: 'I hope this helps'

### 7. Deflate claims of significance

1 places · fix · rule: `inflated`

**Why it is a problem:** When everything is pivotal, the reader cannot tell what actually is.

**How to fix it:**

1. Ask the sentence: why does this matter, and to whom?
2. If there is an answer, write that instead. If not, delete the phrase.

**Where:**

- line 1: 'plays a crucial role'

### 8. Turn nominalisations back into verbs

1 places · fix · rule: `nominalisation`

**Why it is a problem:** 'Make a decision', 'conduct an analysis' take three words to say what one verb says.

**How to fix it:**

1. Find the verb hidden in the noun ('analysis' → 'analyse').
2. Rewrite with that verb and drop the empty verb.
3. Name who does the action.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'make a decision to'

### 9. Name your sources

1 places · fix · rule: `vague-attribution`

**Why it is a problem:** An unnamed authority cannot be checked, so it is not evidence.

**How to fix it:**

1. Find the study, author or dataset you had in mind.
2. Cite it by name and year.
3. If there is none, delete the claim or mark it as your own view.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'Studies show'

### 10. Replace buzzwords with specifics

3 places · consider · rule: `buzzword`

**Why it is a problem:** 'Robust', 'holistic', 'landscape' fit anything, so they describe nothing in particular.

**How to fix it:**

1. Ask: robust against what? comprehensive in which respect?
2. Write the answer in place of the word, or cut the word.
3. A term of art in your field can stay.

**Where:**

- line 1: 'crucial'
- line 1: 'robust'
- line 3: 'cornerstone'

### 11. Use plain 'is' and 'has'

1 places · consider · rule: `copula-avoidance`

**Why it is a problem:** 'Serves as', 'stands as', 'boasts' dress up what 'is' or 'has' says directly.

**How to fix it:**

1. Swap: 'serves as / functions as / stands as' → 'is'; 'features / boasts / offers' → 'has'.
2. Keep the longer verb only where it adds meaning.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'serve as a'

### 12. Thin out the hedges

1 places · consider · rule: `hedging`

**Why it is a problem:** After several stacked hedges no claim is left.

**How to fix it:**

1. Decide how sure you are.
2. Keep one hedge ('probably'), delete the rest.
3. If there is a reason for the uncertainty, name it.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'could potentially'

### 13. Simplify 'not only… but also'

1 places · consider · rule: `not-only-but`

**Why it is a problem:** Used for emphasis it wears thin; the two items usually just add up.

**How to fix it:**

1. If the items are equal, join them with 'and'.
2. Keep it only where the second item is a real surprise after the first.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'not only efficient but also'

### 14. Thin out sentence-opening transitions

1 places · consider · rule: `transition-density`

**Why it is a problem:** A run of 'Moreover', 'Furthermore', 'Additionally' flattens an argument into a list.

**How to fix it:**

1. Delete the opener and check the link is still clear.
2. Where there is real contrast or consequence, keep it marked, preferably mid-sentence.

**Where:**

- line 3: 'Moreover,'

### 15. Review three-item lists

1 places · consider · rule: `triad-density`

**Why it is a problem:** Mechanical prose lists in threes for rhythm even when one or two items matter.

**How to fix it:**

1. For each list ask: do all three items mean different things?
2. Merge near-synonyms and cut the weakest.
3. Where there really are three things, keep the list.

**Where:**

- line 1: 'tools, robust frameworks, and seamless workflows'
